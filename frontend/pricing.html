UNDERSTANDING THE WORKSPACE

Three utilities.
Three purposes.
One organised system.

Minutes support transcription workflows. Bids support participation in eligible job opportunities. Memberships define a user's access to specified platform benefits. Understanding the difference helps everyone use PrimeTask Workspace responsibly.

THE LOGIC BEHIND THE SYSTEM

Why does a digital workspace need utilities?

PrimeTask Workspace is intended to support several kinds of digital work. Different activities require different resources, permissions and workflows. A person handling transcription does not necessarily need the same resources as a person applying for a client assignment. Someone with a membership may also have access to features that are separate from both of those activities.

This is why the platform distinguishes between minutes, bids and memberships. Each has a specific purpose, and each should be explained before a user makes a purchase.

The goal is to make the platform easier to understand. Users should be able to see what they are buying, what the purchase allows them to do, which rules apply and what the purchase does not guarantee.

These utilities must also be managed carefully behind the scenes. A displayed balance is not enough by itself. The system needs reliable records of purchases, usage, access, changes and relevant transactions.

In PrimeTask Workspace, the intended approach is to keep these resources distinct so that a minute balance cannot accidentally become a bid balance, a membership cannot be mistaken for cash, and a payment cannot be treated as successful merely because a browser displayed a success message.

Clear purpose. Clear records. Clear expectations.

Every utility should have a defined role in the user experience and a reliable record of how it is used.

UTILITY 01

Minutes measure audio capacity.

A minute balance tells the system how much eligible transcription capacity is available under the applicable rules.

Explore Transcription

Why does PrimeTask use minutes?

Audio has a measurable duration, and transcription workflows need a consistent way to account for the audio being processed.

1. Minutes represent a measurable service unit

A recording can be measured in minutes and seconds. Using audio duration as a service unit makes it easier to understand how much material a transcription workflow involves.

A minute balance is therefore intended to represent eligible transcription capacity. It is not a financial balance, an earnings balance or a promise that a particular assignment will be available.

2. Minutes help manage service usage

When a transcription workflow involves audio, the platform needs a reliable way to determine whether the required capacity is available. A properly implemented minute wallet can support those checks before an eligible assignment is accepted or processed.

  • ✓ Check whether the required minute balance is available.
  • ✓ Record the minutes associated with a specific assignment.
  • ✓ Prevent the same available minutes from being used twice.
  • ✓ Show the user a clear record of relevant usage and changes.

3. What happens when an assignment uses minutes?

A robust workflow should distinguish between reserving minutes, consuming minutes and releasing a reservation. These are different events and should not be treated as if they were identical.

Example of an assignment workflow

When an eligible assignment is accepted, the required minutes can be reserved so that they are not allocated elsewhere. If the work is approved under the applicable rules, the reserved minutes can be consumed. If the work is rejected and the rules provide for release, the reservation can be returned to the available balance.

This separation helps prevent accidental double-use and gives the platform a clearer record of what happened to the balance. The precise treatment must follow the published service rules and the actual implementation.

4. Why buy minutes?

Purchasing minutes is intended for users who need additional transcription capacity beyond what is otherwise available to them. The purchase should clearly identify the number of minutes being acquired, the applicable price and any restrictions or expiry conditions before payment is confirmed.

The purpose is to make the service unit visible and measurable. A user should not have to guess how much audio capacity a package contains or discover important conditions only after purchasing it.

5. What minutes do not mean

  • ! Minutes are not cash and cannot automatically be treated as money.
  • ! Buying minutes does not guarantee that a client will award a job.
  • ! Minutes do not automatically represent worker earnings.
  • ! A membership should not silently create minute credits unless its published benefits explicitly provide for them.
Important distinction

Audio duration, the amount a user pays for service capacity and the compensation a worker may earn are separate concepts. They must be defined independently rather than treated as the same calculation.

UTILITY 02

Bids support job participation.

Where a service uses a bidding system, bids provide a defined resource for submitting eligible applications or proposals.

Explore Services

Why does a platform need bids?

When clients publish work and workers apply for it, the platform needs a clear process for handling applications.

1. Bids are connected to participation

In a bidding-based workflow, an eligible worker may submit a proposal or express interest in a listed assignment. A bid unit can be used to regulate or measure that activity, depending on the rules of the particular service.

This is different from transcription minutes. Minutes measure audio capacity. Bids are intended to support participation in eligible job opportunities.

2. Bids help structure job applications

A defined bidding system can help the platform manage application activity and apply consistent rules. For example, the system may require an eligible user to have sufficient bid units before submitting a proposal to a job that uses bidding.

The platform must explain which jobs require bids, how many units an application uses, whether a bid can be withdrawn, and whether any return or refund conditions apply. Those rules should be visible before the user confirms an action.

3. Why purchase bids?

A bid purchase is intended for users who need additional participation capacity under an eligible bidding workflow. The purchase should identify the quantity, price, applicable conditions and any limits on use.

Bid purchases should not be presented as payments for guaranteed work. They are intended to support participation, not determine whether a client selects a particular applicant.

4. A bid is not a job award

  • ✓ A bid may allow a user to submit an eligible proposal.
  • ✓ A client or authorised reviewer may still need to evaluate applications.
  • ✓ Assignment selection should follow the relevant job rules.
  • ! Purchasing more bids does not guarantee selection, earnings or a minimum number of assignments.

Why this distinction matters

A user should understand that they are purchasing participation capacity, not purchasing a client's decision. Job availability, requirements, competition and selection criteria remain separate from the number of bid units held by an applicant.

5. Bids should have their own records

Bid purchases and usage should be recorded independently from minutes and membership status. If a proposal uses a bid, the platform should record that event and the relevant job or application reference.

This makes it easier to explain balance changes, investigate disputes and prevent duplicate deductions. It also helps the platform distinguish a payment that successfully purchased bids from a proposal that was actually submitted.

UTILITY 03

Memberships define access and benefits.

A membership is a plan that determines which specified features or services a user is entitled to access under its published conditions. It is not the same thing as buying a consumable utility.

1. Why have memberships?

A growing digital platform may offer different service levels, tools or support arrangements. Memberships can organise those entitlements into understandable plans so that users know what is included and what remains separate.

For example, a plan could define access to particular platform features, learning materials, support channels or service categories. The exact benefits depend on the actual plan approved by PrimeTask Workspace.

2. Memberships are about entitlement

A membership should answer a basic question: what is this user entitled to access under this plan?

That entitlement might be limited by the plan, the service, an activation date, an expiry date or other clearly published conditions. The platform should show the user the plan name, its price, the included benefits, its duration where applicable, and how renewal or cancellation works.

3. Why separate memberships from minutes and bids?

Because access rights and usage balances solve different problems. A membership may permit access to a service, while minutes measure transcription capacity and bids support eligible job applications.

Keeping them separate makes it possible to explain exactly what was purchased. If a plan includes a specific quantity of minutes or bids, that benefit should be explicitly stated and credited according to the plan's rules. It should not be assumed.

4. Memberships must have clear boundaries

  • ✓ List the features and services included in the plan.
  • ✓ Explain the activation period and expiry conditions where applicable.
  • ✓ Show renewal, cancellation and refund rules before purchase.
  • ✓ Explain which benefits require a separate purchase.
  • ! Do not imply that membership automatically guarantees a job, a client or an income.

Understanding membership levels.

The following cards explain possible ways to organise access. They are illustrative categories, not a published price list or a claim that these specific plans are currently available.

ACCESS LEVEL A

Essential Access

A plan designed around a defined set of basic features and resources available to eligible members.

  • Clearly listed account features
  • Access to specified resources
  • Published eligibility conditions
  • Visible plan status and duration
ACCESS LEVEL B

Expanded Access

A plan that may include additional tools or service access beyond the benefits of a basic plan.

  • Benefits listed before checkout
  • Clearly defined service permissions
  • Transparent limits and conditions
  • Separate tracking of purchased utilities
ACCESS LEVEL C

Specialised Access

A plan organised around specific tools, services or support arrangements for eligible users.

  • Defined specialised features
  • Explicit eligibility requirements
  • Clear activation and expiry rules
  • No implied guarantee of paid work

Important: Membership levels only have meaning when their benefits are defined and implemented. A higher price should correspond to clearly stated additional value, not vague promises about future earnings or guaranteed access to work.

THE PURCHASE PROCESS

From selecting a package to confirmed access.

A purchase should be a traceable process, not a disappearing button followed by a mysterious balance change.

01

Select the product

Choose minutes, bids or a membership. The platform should display the selected product, quantity or benefits, price, and applicable conditions.

02

Review and pay

Confirm the order and use the available payment method. The displayed order should match the product and amount actually submitted for payment.

03

Verify the payment

The backend must verify the payment result before treating the order as paid. A browser redirect alone is not sufficient proof of payment.

04

Apply the correct benefit

Credit the purchased utility or activate the purchased membership according to the verified order and its rules.

05

Record the transaction

Save the order reference, payment reference, product, amount, status and resulting balance or membership change.

06

Show confirmation

Display a clear receipt or transaction status so the user can understand what was purchased and whether it has been successfully applied.

FINANCIAL CLARITY

Purchased utilities are not earnings.

A trustworthy platform must distinguish between a user buying something, a worker completing an assignment and a payment becoming eligible for withdrawal.

Four records that should never be confused

PrimeTask Workspace should maintain appropriate, separate records for financial transactions, purchased utilities, membership entitlements and work-related earnings.

1. Purchase record

Records the user's order, the product selected, the amount charged and the verified payment result.

2. Utility balance

Records minutes or bids acquired, used, reserved, released or otherwise adjusted under the applicable rules.

3. Membership record

Records the plan, included benefits, activation status and relevant start or expiry dates.

4. Earnings record

Records eligible compensation for work under the relevant assignment and approval rules. Earnings should not be created merely because a user purchased a utility.

Payments must be confirmed by the server.

A reliable payment integration verifies payment notifications, protects against duplicate processing and updates the correct record. The platform should not credit minutes, bids or membership benefits solely because a client-side page says a transaction succeeded.
