01

Introduction and scope

PrimeTask Workspace ("PrimeTask", "we", "us" or "our") is committed to handling personal information responsibly. This Privacy Policy describes how personal data may be processed in connection with our website, account systems, service workflows, customer support, assignments and other platform features that we make available.

This policy applies to visitors, registered users, workers, clients, prospective members, business contacts and other individuals whose personal information we process in connection with PrimeTask Workspace.

It should be read together with our Terms of Service, any applicable assignment agreement, payment disclosures, consent notice and other notices presented at the point of collection. Where a specific notice explains a particular processing activity in greater detail, that notice should be considered alongside this policy.

1.1 What this policy covers

  • Information submitted through registration, login, contact and support forms.
  • Account, profile, access, job, assignment and submission information.
  • Payment verification records, platform transactions and financial ledgers.
  • Technical logs, security events and limited website usage information.
  • Communications involving users, clients, workers, administrators and support.
  • Rights, retention, disclosures, safeguards and procedures concerning personal data.

1.2 What this policy does not promise

This policy does not guarantee that a website can never experience an incident, that every message will receive an immediate response, or that every service will be available continuously. We must not claim that information is encrypted, deleted automatically, stored only in Kenya, or inaccessible to administrators unless our systems and actual practices substantiate that claim.

03

Categories of information we may collect

We aim to collect information that is relevant and reasonably necessary for a specified purpose. The exact information collected depends on which features a person uses and how the platform is implemented.

3.1 Identity and profile information

  • Name or display name.
  • Username and registered email address.
  • Telephone number where provided or needed for a disclosed purpose.
  • Account type, membership status and selected preferences.
  • Profile information voluntarily submitted by the user.

We should not collect identity documents, sensitive personal data or additional identification details merely because they might be useful someday. Any such collection must have a defined purpose, appropriate safeguards and a lawful basis.

3.2 Account and authentication information

We may process password hashes, login timestamps, session identifiers, verification status, failed login attempts, access logs and security-related account events. Properly designed systems should not store passwords in readable plain text. Passwords and authentication secrets should never be requested through ordinary support messages.

3.3 Work, job and assignment information

  • Job listings, task instructions, bids, applications and assignment identifiers.
  • Submitted transcripts, notes, data-entry files and academic support materials.
  • Assignment status, review comments, quality checks and delivery timestamps.
  • Relevant client-worker communications and dispute records.
  • Information needed to investigate suspected abuse, fraud or policy violations.

3.4 Financial and transaction information

Depending on the implemented payment workflow, we may maintain transaction references, requested purchase details, amounts, currency, payment status, provider response identifiers, refunds, account credits, earnings records and payout status. We should avoid storing complete payment credentials when a payment provider can securely handle them.

Financial records must be accurate and distinguish separate concepts such as purchases, platform utilities, memberships, job earnings and withdrawals. A balance displayed on a screen must not be treated as proof that a payment settled or that a withdrawal was paid.

3.5 Technical and device information

Servers and security systems may record IP addresses, browser type, operating system, request timestamps, error logs, session information and security events. We should limit these records to operational, security and analytical needs, establish retention periods, and restrict access.

3.6 Communications and support information

We may process messages, support requests, complaint details, attachments and correspondence needed to respond to enquiries, maintain records, investigate incidents or resolve disputes. Users should avoid including passwords, payment PINs, unnecessary identity documents or unrelated sensitive information.

3.7 Information we should not collect by default

  • Account passwords in readable form.
  • M-Pesa PINs, bank passwords or one-time verification codes.
  • Precise location data unless a specific, disclosed feature genuinely requires it.
  • Biometric data or sensitive personal data without a justified lawful basis and appropriate safeguards.
  • Information unrelated to the purpose for which it was collected.
04

How information is collected

4.1 Directly from you

We may collect information when you register, log in, update your profile, contact support, apply for a job, accept an assignment, upload work, submit a complaint, request a withdrawal or otherwise use a platform feature.

4.2 Automatically through the platform

Basic technical records may be generated when your browser or device communicates with our servers. Depending on configuration, these may include timestamps, diagnostic logs, session data and security events. Optional analytics and non-essential cookies should not be activated contrary to applicable consent rules.

4.3 From clients and other users

A client may provide task instructions, source files, feedback or assignment information concerning a worker. A worker may provide submissions, questions or delivery information concerning a client project. Access should be limited to individuals who need the information for a legitimate platform purpose.

4.4 From payment and verification providers

A payment provider may return transaction references, payment status, timestamps or other limited information needed to verify a transaction. The exact data depends on the provider and integration. We must document the real fields received and must not imply that a payment was confirmed merely because a user returned to a success page.

4.5 From public or authorised sources

Where necessary and lawful, we may receive limited information from authorised business, fraud-prevention or verification sources. We should explain relevant checks where required by law and avoid obtaining information through unauthorised access or deceptive methods.

05

Purposes for processing personal information

Depending on the feature used and the applicable lawful basis, information may be processed to:

  • Create, administer, authenticate and secure user accounts.
  • Provide requested services and manage assignments.
  • Enable authorised communication between clients, workers and support.
  • Track assignment status, submission history, reviews and delivery records.
  • Verify payments, maintain transaction records and investigate discrepancies.
  • Calculate and record legitimate earnings, fees, credits or withdrawals.
  • Prevent unauthorised access, spam, fraud, manipulation and abuse.
  • Provide customer support, resolve disputes and maintain complaint records.
  • Maintain backups, diagnose errors and improve reliability and accessibility.
  • Meet applicable legal, regulatory, accounting or record-keeping obligations.
  • Communicate important policy, security, service or transaction notices.
  • Evaluate service quality and improve workflows using appropriate safeguards.

5.1 Purpose limitation

We should not reuse personal data for a materially different purpose without assessing compatibility, identifying a lawful basis and providing additional information or obtaining consent where required.

5.2 No sale of personal data as a business model

PrimeTask should not sell personal information as a business model. Any disclosure to service providers or other recipients must have a legitimate, documented purpose and comply with applicable law. The exact practices of the operating business must match this statement before it is published as a final commitment.

07

Assignments, audio recordings and client materials

Transcription, note-taking, data entry and academic writing support may involve information supplied by clients or contained in source files. Some files may include personal, confidential, commercial, educational or otherwise sensitive material.

7.1 Access to source materials

  • Assignment files should be accessible only to authorised people with a legitimate need.
  • Workers should use source materials only for the authorised task.
  • Files should not be copied, downloaded, forwarded or retained outside the permitted workflow.
  • Users must follow any applicable confidentiality and client instructions.
  • Source material should not be posted publicly or used for unrelated demonstrations.

7.2 Audio, video and meeting content

Recordings may contain names, voices, opinions, business discussions, classroom content or other personal information. People who provide recordings must have the authority and any necessary permissions to share and process them. PrimeTask should not encourage unlawful recording or unauthorised disclosure.

Workers should not create independent recordings of clients, meetings, lectures or conversations unless explicitly authorised and lawful. Any recording feature introduced by the platform must be explained separately, including its purpose, access, storage and deletion arrangements.

7.3 Human review and quality assurance

Where an authorised reviewer checks a submission, that reviewer may need access to the source file, submission, comments or quality information. Such access should be restricted to the review purpose. The platform should disclose any material use of external reviewers or third-party tools where required.

7.4 Prohibited reuse

Client recordings, transcripts, notes and documents should not be used to train external artificial intelligence systems, publish examples, create portfolios, or develop unrelated products unless the necessary rights and lawful basis exist and appropriate disclosure or permission has been obtained.

7.5 Accidental disclosure

If a worker believes a source file was sent to the wrong person, exposed publicly or accessed without authorisation, the worker should promptly report the issue through the designated support channel and avoid further sharing.

08

Payments, purchases, earnings and financial records

The platform may process transaction and financial records associated with purchases, account utilities, memberships, eligible work, fees or withdrawals. These records serve different purposes and should not be combined into a single unexplained balance.

8.1 Payment verification

  • Payment status should be verified through the payment provider or a trusted server-side mechanism.
  • A browser redirect or screenshot alone should not be treated as conclusive payment proof.
  • Duplicate callbacks should be handled idempotently so a single transaction is not credited twice.
  • Failed, pending, reversed and completed transactions should be distinguished accurately.
  • Only the minimum payment data necessary for verification and record keeping should be retained.

8.2 Currency and ledger accuracy

Financial records should preserve the actual currency, amount and transaction meaning. The platform must not silently relabel a value from one currency as another. Where payment rails require a different settlement currency, the actual processing and display rules must be documented accurately.

8.3 Earnings and withdrawals

Job rewards, earned amounts, platform credits, membership entitlements and withdrawal records should be maintained separately. A displayed earning is not necessarily proof that a payout has been initiated or completed. Withdrawal records should accurately reflect their real status and relevant transaction references.

8.4 Provider data

Payment service providers may independently process personal data under their own terms and privacy notices. Users should review the relevant provider's information where they interact directly with its service.

8.5 Financial record retention

Certain transaction records may need to be retained for accounting, dispute resolution, fraud prevention or legal compliance even after an account is closed. The actual retention period must be established based on applicable requirements, not invented for the sake of this document.

09

Cookies, local storage and analytics

Cookies and similar technologies may help a website maintain sessions, remember preferences, support security or understand service performance. The actual technologies used by PrimeTask must be inventoried before this section is treated as a complete description.

9.1 Strictly necessary technologies

Technologies required for essential functions, such as maintaining a secure login session, may be used where permitted by applicable law. They should not be used as a pretext for unrelated tracking.

9.2 Optional analytics and marketing technologies

Optional analytics, advertising pixels or cross-site tracking technologies should be disclosed and handled in accordance with applicable consent requirements. If these tools are not used, the policy should not claim that they are.

9.3 Browser storage

Websites may use local storage or session storage to remember interface state or limited preferences. Sensitive authentication tokens should be protected through an appropriate security design. Client-side storage must not be treated as a trusted source of payment confirmation, account balances or permissions.

9.4 Managing preferences

Users can review their browser settings to manage or delete cookies and local website data. Blocking essential technologies may cause some features to stop working. Where a consent manager is required, its available controls must be clearly presented and functional.

10

When information may be shared

Personal information should be disclosed only where there is a valid purpose, an appropriate legal basis and suitable safeguards. We do not treat a person's registration as permission to publish their personal information.

10.1 Clients and workers

Information necessary to perform an assignment may be shared between an authorised client and worker. The information shared should be limited to what is reasonably necessary for the task, communication or dispute process.

10.2 Service providers

Providers may support hosting, databases, file storage, email delivery, security, customer support, analytics or payment processing. The actual providers used should be documented, and appropriate contractual and security arrangements should be in place where required.

10.3 Administrators and authorised personnel

Authorised personnel may access relevant records to administer the platform, review assignments, resolve disputes, investigate security incidents or comply with legal obligations. Access should be role-based, logged where appropriate, periodically reviewed and revoked when no longer needed.

10.4 Legal requests and protection of rights

We may disclose information where required by law or valid legal process, or where disclosure is otherwise permitted and reasonably necessary to protect rights, safety, property or platform integrity. Requests should be assessed for authenticity, scope and legal validity.

10.5 Business transfers

If the business undergoes a merger, restructuring, sale or transfer, relevant information may be transferred where lawful and subject to appropriate safeguards and notices. We should not use this clause to justify unrelated disclosures.

10.6 Public disclosure

We should not publicly display private email addresses, telephone numbers, personal documents, payment details or private communications without an appropriate legal basis or valid permission.

International transfers and hosting locations

Digital services may use infrastructure located in countries other than the country where a user lives. The location of a website's domain or its business operator does not, by itself, establish where its databases, backups, emails or uploaded files are stored.

If personal data is transferred outside Kenya or another relevant jurisdiction, PrimeTask must assess applicable transfer requirements and implement required safeguards. Depending on the circumstances, this may involve contractual protections, adequacy arrangements, consent or another legally recognised route.

11.1 Information that must be verified

  • Primary hosting and database regions.
  • File-storage and backup locations.
  • Email and support-provider processing locations.
  • Payment-provider processing arrangements.
  • Any international access by authorised support or engineering personnel.

PrimeTask must not promise that all personal data remains within Kenya unless the actual infrastructure and provider arrangements support that statement.

12

Security safeguards and access control

We should implement technical and organisational measures appropriate to the nature, volume, sensitivity and risks of the personal data processed. No online system can promise absolute security, but that is not an excuse for neglecting basic safeguards.

12.1 Expected safeguards

  • HTTPS for website traffic and secure transport where supported.
  • Secure password hashing and sensible authentication controls.
  • Role-based access permissions for users, administrators and reviewers.
  • Server-side validation of sensitive operations and account permissions.
  • Payment verification through trusted server-side provider responses.
  • Protection of API credentials, signing secrets and environment variables.
  • Access logging and monitoring appropriate to system risk.
  • Secure backups, recovery planning and controlled restoration procedures.
  • Dependency updates, vulnerability management and testing before releases.
  • Documented processes for handling privacy requests and security incidents.

12.2 Administrator responsibilities

Administrative access must be limited to authorised individuals. Shared passwords, public credential files, unrestricted database access and hard-coded production secrets increase the risk of unauthorised access. Privileged accounts should use stronger authentication where available.

12.3 User responsibilities

Users should keep their login details confidential, use a secure device, log out of shared devices, and report suspected account compromise. Users should not attempt to access another person's files, session, account or private messages.

12.4 Limits of security

We cannot guarantee that every internet transmission, device, third-party system or service will remain free from risk. Where a security incident occurs, we will assess the circumstances and take appropriate steps consistent with applicable law.

13

Data retention, account closure and deletion

Personal data should not be retained indefinitely without a purpose. Retention should reflect operational needs, contractual requirements, security considerations and applicable legal obligations.

13.1 Retention principles

  • Keep information only for as long as a documented purpose requires.
  • Apply separate retention rules to account profiles, assignment files, financial records and security logs.
  • Review records that have reached the end of their retention period.
  • Delete, anonymise or securely dispose of information when appropriate.
  • Limit backup retention and access, and document how deleted records age out of backups.

Users may request account deletion through the designated contact channel, subject to appropriate identity verification. Deletion may not immediately remove every record where retention is required by law, necessary for a valid dispute, or otherwise permitted under applicable law. Any continuing retention should be limited to the relevant purpose.

13.4 Deactivation is not necessarily deletion

Disabling an account may prevent login without deleting historical records. The platform must explain the difference and implement the selected action accurately. It should not describe an account as deleted if identifiable information remains in active systems without a valid reason.

14

Your privacy rights and how to exercise them

Subject to applicable law and the circumstances of the request, individuals may have rights concerning their personal information. The precise scope and applicable deadlines must be assessed under the law governing the processing.

14.1 Potential rights

  • Information: receive clear information about relevant processing.
  • Access: request access to personal data held about you.
  • Correction: request correction of inaccurate or incomplete data.
  • Deletion: request erasure where the applicable legal conditions are met.
  • Objection: object to certain processing where the law provides that right.
  • Restriction: request restriction of certain processing where available.
  • Consent withdrawal: withdraw consent where processing relies on consent.
  • Portability: request data portability where the right applies.
  • Complaint: raise a concern with the relevant supervisory authority.

14.2 How to make a request

  1. Contact the privacy team using the designated privacy contact published on this page.
  2. Describe the request and identify the relevant account or processing activity.
  3. Provide only the information reasonably needed to verify your identity.
  4. Respond to reasonable clarification requests if necessary.
  5. Keep a copy of the request and any reference supplied.

14.3 Identity verification

We may need to verify the identity or authority of a requester before disclosing personal information or changing an account. Verification should be proportionate. We should not routinely request excessive documents or collect new sensitive information where a safer, less intrusive method is available.

14.4 Response periods and limitations

Requests will be handled within the period required by applicable law. Some requests may be refused or limited where a lawful exception applies, such as protecting another person's rights or meeting a legal retention obligation. Where required, we will explain the outcome and any available complaint route.

14.5 No retaliation for lawful requests

A person should not be penalised merely for exercising a valid privacy right. However, certain information may remain necessary to provide an active service, complete a transaction or comply with legal requirements.

15

Children and age-related safeguards

PrimeTask should establish and publish a clear minimum-age policy appropriate to its services, contractual arrangements and applicable law. If a service is not intended for children, the registration flow and service design should communicate that restriction clearly.

We should not knowingly collect children's personal data in circumstances where doing so would be unlawful. Where the law requires parental or guardian consent, age assurance or additional safeguards, those requirements must be implemented before processing begins.

If we become aware that information has been collected unlawfully from a child, we will assess the situation and take appropriate action, which may include restricting processing, contacting an authorised guardian where lawful, or deleting the information where appropriate.

Users should not upload identifiable information about children into assignments unless the task is authorised, the sharing is lawful and the necessary safeguards are in place.

Third-party websites, providers and integrations

16.1 External links

16.3 Changes in providers

We may send messages necessary to administer an account or requested service, such as account verification, security alerts, assignment updates, payment confirmations or important policy changes, where permitted by law.

18

Automated processing and platform decisions

Software may automatically validate forms, calculate balances, route assignments, flag unusual activity or determine whether required fields are complete. These functions should be tested for accuracy and appropriate access controls.

Where a decision is made solely by automated processing and has legal or similarly significant effects on a person, additional legal requirements may apply. PrimeTask must assess those requirements before introducing such a feature.

Where required, individuals should receive meaningful information about the relevant processing and have access to the rights or review mechanisms provided by applicable law.

18.1 No unsupported claims

We should not claim that the platform uses artificial intelligence, automated profiling or algorithmic job selection unless those systems are actually used. If they are introduced later, this section should be reviewed and updated.

19

Security incidents and personal data breaches

A suspected incident involving personal data must be assessed promptly. Examples include unauthorised access, accidental disclosure, lost devices containing personal data, compromised credentials, exposed files or an unintended database exposure.

19.1 Response process

  1. Record the incident and preserve relevant evidence.
  2. Restrict unauthorised access and contain the incident where reasonably possible.
  3. Assess what information was involved, whose data may be affected and the likely risks.
  4. Secure affected accounts, systems, credentials or files as appropriate.
  5. Determine whether notification to individuals or authorities is required.
  6. Make required notifications within applicable legal deadlines.
  7. Document the response, corrective actions and lessons learned.

19.2 Notifications

Where applicable law requires notification to a supervisory authority or affected individuals, PrimeTask will follow the relevant requirements and deadlines. Notification obligations depend on the circumstances, the role of the parties, the risk to individuals and the governing law.

19.3 Reporting a suspected incident

Users should report suspected account compromise or unintended disclosure promptly through the official support channel. Include a concise description, approximate time and relevant reference. Do not publicly post exposed personal data or send passwords and verification codes in the report.

Definitions

Personal data: Information relating to an identified or identifiable individual.
Data subject: The individual to whom personal data relates.
Data controller: A person or organisation that determines the purposes and essential means of processing personal data, as defined by applicable law.
Data processor: A person or organisation that processes personal data on behalf of a controller, as defined by applicable law.
Processing: An operation performed on personal data, such as collecting, recording, organising, storing, using, disclosing, restricting or deleting it.
Consent: A person's agreement to a specified processing activity where that agreement meets the requirements of applicable law.
Personal data breach: A security incident leading to accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to personal data, as defined by applicable law.
Service provider: An external organisation engaged to support an operational function such as hosting, payment processing, storage or communications.
Assignment materials: Source files, recordings, instructions, notes, transcripts, data-entry records or other materials supplied or created in connection with a task.
Platform account: A user profile and associated records used to access PrimeTask Workspace features.
